Why this isn't the full picture

A calculator gives you a number. A dietitian gives you a plan.

This estimate uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the same starting formula our dietitians use — but it doesn't know your medical history, food preferences, or what's actually realistic for your week.

If you have diabetes, PCOS, thyroid concerns, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, these numbers can be significantly off. Talk to a dietitian before using them to guide meal planning.
